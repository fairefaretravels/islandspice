# AI Saneca — Day 3 Lesson Script
### Challenge 3: Build Something You Need
*(~30 minutes of spoken dialogue)*

---

## 1. HOOK

Most people are sitting on an entire week's worth of work that could actually get done in one afternoon — and they don't know it yet.

Not because they're lazy. Not because they're bad at their job. Because nobody ever showed them that the boring, repetitive part of work — the emails, the documents, the spreadsheets — is exactly the part AI is built to help with.

Today we're clearing real work. Not theoretical work. The stuff actually sitting in your inbox right now.

---

## 2. STORY

I want to tell you about Danielle. Danielle runs a small cleaning service. Every single week, she's got the same handful of emails to answer — "do you service my area," "how much for a two-bedroom," "can you come Saturday" — over and over, slightly different words, same basic questions.

Danielle used to sit down every Sunday night and answer twenty of these one by one, from scratch, every time. Two hours, every week, just typing variations of the same answer.

One Sunday, she tried something different. She showed AI three real emails she'd already sent — her actual words, her actual tone — and said, "here's five new ones, answer them the way I would." Fifteen minutes. Not two hours. Fifteen minutes, and they sounded like her, because she gave it her actual voice to copy, not a vague instruction to "sound professional."

That's the whole shift. She stopped asking it to guess her tone and started showing it her tone.

---

## 3. LESSON

Here's today's plain-English lesson: **work is repetitive in ways we stop noticing — and AI is built for repetition with variation.**

The mistake most people make is telling AI to "sound professional" or "sound friendly" — and getting back something generic, because those words don't mean anything specific. What actually works is showing it two or three real examples of what "good" looks like, in your own words, and asking it to match that.

**Examples beat instructions. Every time.**

So today's skill isn't "write a good prompt." It's "show, don't just tell." Give it a sample of your real voice, your real standard, and let it match that pattern across a whole batch of new work.

---

## 4. DEMO

Let's clear some real work, live.

**The vague version.**

> "Write a professional email responding to a customer asking about pricing."

Look at what comes back. Fine. Generic. Could be anybody's business, any tone, any industry. Technically "professional." Completely forgettable.

**Now the real version — showing it my actual voice.**

> "Here are two emails I've actually sent to customers before: [paste two real examples]. Using that same tone, respond to this new message: [paste the new customer question]."

Look at the gap. This one sounds like an actual person now — because it is, it's just working off real examples instead of a vague vibe.

**Now let's clear your actual queue.**

Pick a real category sitting in your work right now — emails, a document, or a spreadsheet formula you keep redoing by hand. Give it:

1. Two or three real examples of your own past work in that category
2. The new batch you need done
3. The instruction to match your existing standard, not invent a new one

Then — this part matters — you're the editor now, not the writer. Go through fast: accept, tweak, or reject each one. You're not starting from zero anymore, you're reviewing.

Watch me clear a real batch, live, start to finish. If you're clearing your own queue right now, tell me in the chat how much time that just bought you back.

## 5. CHALLENGE

Three questions before you go:

**What surprised you** about how it sounded once you gave it real examples instead of just instructions?

**What worked** — what part of your queue did this actually clear?

**What would you do differently** — what example would you add next time to make it sound even more like you?

Drop your answers below. If your honest answer is "it still didn't quite sound like me," that's useful — that just means we need one more real example next time, not a different tool.
