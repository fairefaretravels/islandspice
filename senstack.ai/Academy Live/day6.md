# AI Saneca — Day 6 Lesson Script
### Challenge 6: Inbox Zero, Sort Of
*(~30 minutes of spoken dialogue)*

---

## 1. HOOK
*(High energy. Smile in your voice before you even say a word.)*

Quick question before we start. When's the last time you opened your inbox and felt calm?

Not "fine." Not "I'll deal with it later." Calm.

If you can't remember — you're not alone, and it's not because you're disorganized. It's because your inbox was never designed to be sorted by a human brain that also has to do a hundred other things today. It was designed to just... keep filling up.

Here's the good news. The skill you already have from Day One — being specific instead of vague — works on your inbox too. Today we're not going to "get to inbox zero." We're going to build something better: a system that sorts itself, in your voice, every single day.

Let's fix this.

---

## 2. STORY

Meet Marcus. Marcus manages a small team, and every morning he opens his laptop to somewhere between 40 and 90 unread emails. Client questions. Internal asks. Newsletters he means to unsubscribe from. Somewhere in there, buried on page two, is the one email that actually needed a reply yesterday.

Marcus tried the obvious fix — he asked AI to "clean up my inbox." It gave him a generic list of tips. Folders. Filters. Unsubscribe. He knew all that already. It didn't help him today, with the actual pile sitting in front of him.

Here's what changed it. Instead of asking for tips, Marcus started handing AI the actual mess — the real subject lines, the real senders, the real half-written replies he'd abandoned — and telling it exactly what "urgent" meant to him, and exactly how he sounds when he writes.

The AI didn't need to be smarter. It needed Marcus to stop describing the problem in general terms and start describing his actual inbox.

Twenty minutes later, Marcus had three buckets and two drafted replies that sounded like him, not like a robot. Same tool everyone else had access to. Different result — because he gave it something real to work with.

---

## 3. LESSON

Same rule as Day One, applied to a new problem:

**Vague in, vague out. Specific in, useful out.**

For your inbox, "specific" breaks down into three things:

1. **Tell it what urgent actually means to you.** Not everyone's "urgent" is the same. Is it anything from a client? Anything with a dollar sign? Anything from your boss? Say it out loud, because AI can't guess your priorities — you have to hand them over.

2. **Give it real examples of your voice.** Paste in two or three emails you've actually sent. Not because AI needs to copy you word for word — because it needs a reference point for how direct, how warm, how long your replies usually run.

3. **Tell it what to do with the leftovers.** Not every email needs a reply today. Some need to wait. Some need to be archived. Decide the buckets before you start sorting, and the whole pile stops feeling infinite.

That's the whole skill: name your priorities, hand over your voice, define your buckets. Do that once, and you can reuse it on every inbox you'll ever have.

---

## 4. DEMO

Let's do this live, on a real mess.

**Step one — the vague version.**

> "Help me clean up my inbox."

*(Pause.)*

Look at that. Generic advice again. Folders, filters, unsubscribe. Not wrong — just not useful for the 60 emails sitting in front of us right now.

**Step two — let's fix it.**

> "Here are ten real subject lines from my inbox right now: [paste them]. Anything from a client or my direct manager is urgent — reply today. Internal FYIs and newsletters can wait or get archived. Sort these into three buckets: reply today, reply this week, archive. For the two client emails, draft short, direct replies in my voice — here's an example of how I usually write: [paste a real email]."

*(Pause.)*

Look at the difference. Real buckets. Real drafts. Something you could send in the next five minutes instead of a lecture about folders.

**Step three — building your standing inbox assistant.**

Here's today's real project — a prompt you save and reuse every morning, the same way you built your personal assistant prompt on Day One.

> "From now on, when I paste in my unread subject lines, sort them into three buckets: reply today, reply this week, archive. Here's what counts as urgent to me: [your real definition]. Here's how I write — direct, warm, short, whatever's true: [describe it, or paste an example]. For anything in 'reply today,' draft a short response in my voice."

That's it. Save it. Paste your real inbox into it tomorrow morning before you do anything else.
