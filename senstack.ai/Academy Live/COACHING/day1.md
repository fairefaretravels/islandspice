# AI Saneca — Day 1 Lesson Script
### Challenge 1: Teach AI About You
*(~30 minutes of spoken dialogue)*

---

## 1. HOOK
*(High energy. Smile in your voice before you even say a word.)*

What if I told you the reason AI feels confusing has nothing to do with you being "bad with technology" — and everything to do with the fact that almost everybody is using it completely backwards?

Yeah. Backwards.

Here's what I mean. Most people open up an AI chat, type something short and vague like "help me with my day," get back some generic, watered-down answer, and think, "Okay, this AI thing is overhyped." And they close the tab and never come back.

I want to save you from that today. Because that's not an AI problem. That's a "nobody taught you how to talk to it" problem. And that's exactly what we're fixing in the next thirty minutes.

You don't need to become an expert today. You don't need to know a single technical word. You just need to become a slightly better thinker — and I promise you already have everything you need for that.

Let's figure this out together.

---

## 2. STORY

I want you to picture somebody — let's call her Renee. Renee runs her own small business. She's also a mom, she's got a household to keep running, a business to keep alive, and about four hours less sleep than she needs on any given night. Sound like anyone you know? Maybe sound like you?

Renee heard AI could "save her time." So she opened it up one night, typed "write me a business plan," got three paragraphs of nothing-flavored corporate mush, and thought, "Well, that was useless." She didn't come back to it for two months.

Here's what nobody told Renee: AI didn't fail her. Her question failed her. She asked it something so broad that it had nothing real to work with — no idea what her business actually does, who her customers are, what she's struggling with, what "good" even looks like to her.

It's like walking up to a brand-new employee on day one, saying "handle the thing," and walking away. Of course that goes badly. Not because the employee is incompetent — because you gave them zero context.

The second time Renee tried it, she did something different. She told it who she was. What her business does. What was actually keeping her up at night. And this time? She walked away with something she could actually use that same afternoon.

Same tool. Same Renee. Completely different result. That's the whole lesson today, wrapped up in one story.

---

## 3. LESSON

Okay, let's slow down and actually name the skill here, in plain English, no jargon.

**AI is a prediction engine, not a mind reader.**

That's it. That's the whole secret. It is really, really good at continuing a thought — but it can only continue the thought you actually give it. If you hand it something vague, it hands you back something generic, because generic is the safest guess it can make with nothing to go on.

So here's the one sentence I want you to remember today:

**Vague in, vague out. Specific in, useful out.**

That's it. That's not technical. That's just... common sense, applied to a new kind of tool.

Now — "specific" doesn't mean complicated. It means three simple things:

1. **Tell it who you are.** Not your resume. Your actual situation. "I'm a mom running a small candle business out of my garage" tells it a hundred times more than "I'm an entrepreneur."

2. **Tell it what you actually need.** Not "help me," but the real thing — "I need to respond to five customer emails and I'm exhausted and don't want to sound cold."

3. **Tell it what 'good' looks like to you.** Short and blunt? Warm and chatty? Bullet points? Say it. It can't read your preferences off your face — you have to say them out loud.

That's the whole skill. Context, specificity, and telling it what success looks like. And here's the beautiful part — this works in whatever AI tool exists right now, and it's going to work in whatever AI tool exists five years from now, because it's not about the tool. It's about how you think before you type.

You just need to know what question to ask next. That's it. That's the whole ballgame.

---

## 4. DEMO

Alright, let's actually do this live, together, right now. No slides, no theory — real problem, real fix.

**Step one — the vague version.**

Watch what happens when I type something totally normal, totally reasonable-sounding, but vague:

> "Help me plan my day."

*(Pause. Let it land.)*

Look at what comes back. It's fine. It's... nothing. Generic wake-up-drink-water-make-a-list stuff. Nothing wrong with it, and nothing useful about it either. This is the AI doing its best with zero information. It's not broken. It's just guessing.

**Step two — let's fix it, live.**

Now watch what happens when I give it something real instead:

> "I'm a single parent running a small Etsy shop selling handmade jewelry. Today I've got two kids' pickups, one custom order I'm behind on, and I haven't eaten a real meal in two days because I keep skipping lunch to catch up on messages. Help me build a realistic schedule for today that gets the order done, doesn't ignore my kids, and actually includes eating. Keep it short — bullet points, no fluff."

*(Pause.)*

Look at the difference. This isn't a generic productivity list anymore. This is a plan built around a real human being with real constraints. That's not a smarter AI. That's the exact same AI — I just gave it something to actually work with.

**Step three — building your personal assistant prompt.**

Here's today's real project. We're going to build something you can reuse every single day — not a one-time trick, a standing assistant that already knows your situation.

Watch how I build mine:

> "From now on, you're my personal assistant. Here's who I am: [your real situation — job, family, what's usually on your plate]. Here's how I like help delivered: [short and direct / warm and encouraging / step-by-step — whatever's true for you]. Every time I paste in what's on my plate for the day, reorganize it by priority, flag anything I'm about to forget, and don't sugarcoat it if I'm overcommitting."

That's it. That's the whole prompt. Notice I didn't write a paragraph of technical instructions — I just told it, like I'd tell a new assistant on their first day, who I am and how I like to work.

Try pasting in your actual day right now and see what it hands back. If you're watching live, drop what it gave you in the chat — I want to see it.

---

## 5. CHALLENGE

Here's your one thing for today. Doesn't need to take more than an hour, and honestly, most of you will be done in fifteen minutes.

**Build your own personal assistant prompt**, using the three ingredients from the lesson:
- Who you are (your real situation, not your resume)
- How you like help delivered
- One standing job you want it to do every time you paste in your day

Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again — notes app, a doc, wherever. Use it tomorrow morning before you do anything else. That's the whole assignment.

No extra credit. No fifty-step homework. One prompt, saved, used once. That's a win today.

---

## 6. REFLECTION

Before you go, I want you to sit with three quick questions — you can answer them in your head, or drop them in the comments, I read every single one:

**What surprised you** about the difference between the vague version and the specific version?

**What worked** when you built your own prompt — what did it get right about you?

**What would you do differently** next time you write a prompt — what's one thing you'll add now that you didn't think to add before?

There's no wrong answer here. If your honest answer is "I didn't think it would actually feel like it knew me," — that's a great answer. That's the whole lesson clicking.

---

## 7. CLOSING

Here's what I want you to walk away with today, and I want you to hear it in my voice every time you open an AI tool from now on:

**Don't chase tools. Learn the skill. The tools will change. The skill stays forever.**

Today's tool might not be the one you're using a year from now. Doesn't matter. What you built today — the habit of giving real context, being specific, saying what "good" looks like — that skill works in literally anything that comes next. That's what actually makes you unafraid of AI. Not memorizing this app. Understanding how to think alongside it.

And I want to say this plainly, because it matters: I am not here to impress you with big words. I'm here to make this feel easy, because it is easy, once somebody actually shows you how to think about it instead of just what button to click.

You don't need to know everything. You just need to know what question to ask next. And after today, you do.
