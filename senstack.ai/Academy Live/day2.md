# AI Saneca — Day 2 Lesson Script
### Challenge 2: Save Yourself One Hour
*(~30 minutes of spoken dialogue)*

---

## 1. HOOK

What if AI could hand you back one full hour this week — not someday, this week — and all it cost you was learning to ask one better question?

Most people think AI is for "big" stuff. Business plans. Coding. Fancy stuff. But honestly? The most valuable thing it's ever done for anybody I've coached is the boring stuff. The planning. The budgeting. The "what's for dinner" stuff that eats up way more of your week than you realize.

Today we're not doing anything fancy. We're doing something practical enough that you'll actually feel it by Friday.

---

## 2. STORY

Let me tell you about Marcus. Marcus works a full-time job, coaches his kid's rec league team on weekends, and somehow is also supposed to remember to pay the light bill, plan meals, and figure out how the family's getting to his sister's birthday three states away next month.

Marcus doesn't need a life coach. Marcus needs somebody to just... hold all the pieces for five minutes so he can see them clearly. That's it. That's the whole problem — not that he's bad at planning, it's that his brain is holding forty tabs open at once and none of them are getting closed.

One night, half-asleep, Marcus dumped his entire week into an AI chat — appointments, budget for the month, what was in the fridge, the trip he needed to plan — all messy, all in one paragraph, typos and all. And it came back and just... organized it. Not perfectly. But enough that Marcus looked at it and said, "Oh. Okay. I can work with this."

That's the whole trick today. You're not trying to impress anybody with a beautiful prompt. You're trying to hand over the mess and let it come back organized.

---

## 3. LESSON

Here's the plain-English version of today's skill: **AI is really good at taking a pile of constraints and turning it into a plan — but only if you actually give it the real constraints.**

Planning, budgeting, meals, travel — four totally different-sounding problems. Same shape underneath. All four are just: "here's what I've got, here's what I need, help me fit it together."

The mistake people make is asking for a plan without giving any of the real numbers. "Help me budget" is vague. "I've got $340 left for groceries this month and four people to feed" — that's specific, and that's what actually gets you something useful.

One more thing I want you to remember, because it'll save you every time: **ask it to check its own plan against your constraints before it hands it to you.** That one move catches almost every mistake before you even see it. It's like asking a contractor to double-check their own measurements before they cut the wood.

That's the whole lesson. Real numbers in. A checked plan out.

---

## 4. DEMO

Let's do this live.

**The vague version first.**

> "Help me plan meals for the week."

Look at what comes back — fine, generic, could be anybody's meal plan. Nothing wrong, nothing useful.

**Now the real version.**

> "I've got about $60 left for groceries this week, three kids who won't eat anything spicy, and I only have about twenty minutes a night to cook after work. Give me five dinners that fit that, and check that the total cost stays under $60 before you give it to me."

Look at the difference. This isn't a Pinterest meal plan anymore. This is built around real dollars, real picky kids, real twenty-minute nights. Same tool. Completely different result, because I gave it something real to work with.

**Now let's build your actual life planner.**

Pick one thing that's genuinely stressing you out this week — budget, meals, or a trip you're trying to plan. Give it:

1. Your real numbers or real constraints — no placeholders, your actual situation
2. What "done" looks like for you this week
3. The ask to double-check its own answer against what you gave it

Watch me build mine live, start to finish — real budget, real plan, checked and handed back. If you're building yours right now, drop it in the chat. I want to see what it built for you.

---

## 5. CHALLENGE

Today's challenge: build one real plan — budget, meals, or a trip — using your actual numbers, not placeholders. Use it this week. That's it.

If it saves you even thirty minutes of mental load this week, that's a win. You don't need to overhaul your whole life today. One plan. One real problem. Done.

---

## 6. REFLECTION

Three quick questions before you go:

**What surprised you** about how it handled your real constraints?

**What worked** — what part of the plan did you actually use?

**What would you do differently** next time — what detail would you add that you left out this time?

Drop your answers in the comments. There's no bad answer here — if your honest answer is "I still don't fully trust it yet," that's honest, and that's fine. Trust builds one good result at a time.
