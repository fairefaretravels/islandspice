Module 2 · Challenge 8

Documents on Demand

The blank page is the hardest part of any report. Today, you never start from blank again.

The Story

The report that sat open for a week

Think about the last time you had to write something official — a report, a proposal, a memo to your boss or your team. Chances are it sat open in a tab for days before you wrote a single line, because starting from a blank page is genuinely one of the hardest parts of any job.

Here's the reframe: you already know what the document needs to say. You've just been stuck on how to say it "properly." That's exactly the gap AI closes — it doesn't know your situation, but it's great at structure once you hand it your actual points.

The people who use this well don't ask AI to "write a report." They dump their real outline — even messy bullet points — and ask it to build the structure around what they already know.

The Lesson

Give it your outline, not just your topic

A topic gets you generic filler. An outline — even three rough bullet points — gets you a document built around what you actually meant to say.

The move: jot your real points first, however messy, then hand them over with the format you need (memo, proposal, report) and one example of your own writing if you have one.

Try It Now

Vague vs. specific

Vague "Write a report about our Q3 performance."

You'll get a generic template with placeholder-sounding sentences.

Specific "Write a one-page internal memo about Q3. My real points: revenue was up 12% but mostly from one client, we lost two smaller accounts, and I want to propose diversifying next quarter. Keep it direct, no corporate filler, and end with the proposal as a clear ask."

Same length request. Completely different document — because now it's building around your actual points instead of guessing what a "Q3 report" usually contains.

Your Challenge Today

Draft one real document

Pick the document you've been avoiding. Write your real points as messy bullets first, then hand them over with the format and tone you need. Edit what comes back.

⏱ Under an hour
Reflection

Before you move on

  • What surprised you about how well it built around your messy bullets?
  • What worked — which of your real points shaped the draft the most?
  • What would you do differently — what would you outline more clearly next time?
