Inbox Zero, Sort Of

Most people either ignore their inbox or drown in it. There's a third option: let AI carry the drafting so you only do the deciding.

The Story

Danielle's Sunday email backlog

Danielle runs a small online shop, and every Sunday night she sits down to answer the customer emails that piled up all week — the same five kinds of questions, over and over: "where's my order," "does this come in blue," "can I return this."

She used to write every single reply from scratch. Same tone, same information, retyped every time. It took her two hours most Sundays.

Here's what changed it: she stopped asking AI to "write a customer email" and started showing it two or three of her own real replies first. Now it drafts in her voice, not a generic customer-service voice — and she's editing, not writing.

The Lesson

Examples beat instructions

You could spend all day describing your tone in words — "friendly but professional, not too formal, a little warm." Or you could just show it two real emails you've actually sent. Showing beats describing, almost every time.

The move: paste in 2-3 examples of how you actually write, then hand over the new message and ask it to match.

Try It Now

Vague vs. specific

Vague "Write a professional email responding to a customer asking about a refund."

Generic. Could be from any business, written by anyone.

Specific — with examples "Here are two emails I've sent before: [paste example 1] [paste example 2]. Now write a reply in that same voice to this message: [paste the new customer email]. Keep it short like my examples, and offer the store credit option before the refund option."

Same AI. Completely different result — because now it has something real to match.

Your Challenge Today

Clear five real emails

Pull up your actual inbox. Give it 2-3 examples of your real writing voice, then run five real messages through the pattern above. Edit what comes back — you're the editor now, not the writer.

⏱ Under an hour — most people finish in 20 minutes
Reflection

Before you move on

  • What surprised you about how close it got to your actual voice?
  • What worked — which example did the most heavy lifting?
  • What would you do differently next time — what would you show it that you didn't this time?
